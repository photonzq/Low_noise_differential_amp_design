# BJT 1/f noise, and BJT against JFET input stages for 1–100 Ω sources: literature notes

Compiled 2026-11-24 for `low_noise_preamp.md` (front ends H: 2 × JFE2140 per side; L: 12 × JFE150 per side at 5 mA). Question: would a bipolar input stage be better for source resistances of about 1–100 Ω, and how does 1/f noise change that comparison? Companion to `literature_notes.md` (entries T8 Leach, D14 AN106, C1–C16 are referred to by those IDs).

**Notation.** Base-current noise $S_{IB}(f)=2qI_B+K_F I_B^{A_F}/f$ (A²/Hz), as in the SPICE Gummel–Poon model, so $K_F$ has units A$^{2-A_F}$; for $A_F=1$, $K_F=fS_{IB,f}/I_B$ in A, the quantity Kleinpenning tabulates (P1). Corner of $i_n$: $f_{ci}=K_FI_B^{A_F-1}/(2q)$ [derived; Leach Eq. (46) for $\beta\to\infty$]. JFET input-referred 1/f: $e_{n,f}^2=K/f$, with $K$ in nV² (i.e. nV²·Hz, the 1/f excess at 1 Hz). $r_{bb'}$ = base spreading resistance (Leach's $r_x$). $r_e=kT/(qI_C)$ ≈ 25.9 Ω at 1 mA, 300 K.

## Short answers to questions (a)–(f)

**(a) Where BJT 1/f noise originates.** In silicon BJTs the dominant low-frequency source is a fluctuation of the **base current**, located in the emitter–base junction region (Kleinpenning P1, pp. 1983, 1990; Bruce et al. P3, p. 998: emitter side of the B–E junction, not $r_b$ or $r_e$). Three locations are documented: (i) the **surface/periphery** of the emitter–base junction (Si–SiO₂ interface over the junction edge; surface recombination): Jaeger and Brodersen (Q2, a surface 1/f source in addition to an active-base source), McDonald (Q3, 1/f tied to surface recombination velocity), Mounib et al. via Kleinpenning (P1, p. 1988: defects at the Si–SiO₂ interface at the periphery of the E–B junction strongly affect base-current noise, barely the collector noise); (ii) the **polysilicon/monocrystalline interface (interfacial oxide)** of poly-emitter devices: Markus and Kleinpenning (P2: noise rises with interfacial-oxide thickness, Fig. 5; homogeneous over the emitter area, Fig. 4), Vempati et al. (Q11), Deen et al. (Q13), Simoen et al. (Q12), Fleetwood (P5, p. 1480: "MOS-like" noise from oxide defects over the B–E junction); (iii) the **bulk** (mobility fluctuations of minority carriers in emitter or base, Hooge model; Kleinpenning P1, Eqs. (19)–(23), (30); Stoisiek and Wolf Q7, resistance fluctuations in the base region). At high currents the **series resistances** $r_b$, $r_e$ add their own 1/f (P1, Eqs. (32)–(34), p. 1986; P2, p. 725). The **collector current** also has a 1/f component (P1, Eq. (30); Mounib, P1 p. 1987), normally much smaller in effect; see (c).

**(b) Scaling of $S_{IB}$.** $S_{IB,f}\propto I_B^{A_F}$ with $A_F=1$ under the mobility-fluctuation model (P1, pp. 1983, 1990; P2, Fig. 3 large devices at low current) and $A_F\approx2$ under number (trapping) fluctuations, typical of poly-emitter interfacial-oxide noise (P1, Eq. (27), p. 1984; P2, Fig. 3; Q11; Q13). Intermediate values are common: 1.7 (SiGe HBT, P3 Fig. 5), 1.6 (GaAs HBT, P1 p. 1990), $1\le\gamma\le2$ (Q7), "1 < m < 3" (Leach P6, p. 1517); [derived] ≈1.2–1.4 for LM394 and 2SB737, ≈1.5–1.7 for 2N5088/2N930 (M2, X1). Area: $S_{IB}/I_B^2\propto1/A_E$ for sources spread uniformly over the emitter (P2, Fig. 4 and Eq. (9); Q11; Q13), with an additional **perimeter** term when the perimeter-to-area ratio is large (Q13) or when surface sources dominate (Q2, Q3). Stoisiek and Wolf give $S_{iB}\propto j_B^{\gamma}F_E^{\beta}w_B^{-1}f^{-1}$, $\beta=1.3$ or 2 (Q7).

**(c) Collector-current 1/f and coherence.** The collector current has its own 1/f (P1, Eq. (30), proportional to $I_C$ in the mobility model; Zhu et al. via P1, Eq. (39): $AI_C+BI_C^4$), but in the measured devices the base-current source dominates. Bruce et al. (P3) measured base-lead and collector-lead noise simultaneously on a SiGe HBT: coherence ≈ 1 from 10 Hz to 10 kHz (Fig. 3, p. 996) and $S_{ICE}\propto R_S^2$ for 300 Ω ≤ $R_S$ ≤ $r_\pi$ (Fig. 4), i.e. a single base-current source; only at low $R_S$ (1–34 Ω) does coherence fall, revealing a second source $S_X=4.4\times10^{-21}$ A²/Hz at 170 Hz, possibly the collector-current noise (p. 995). [derived] For the MAT03 at 1 mA the data-sheet $e_n$(1 Hz) ≈ 1.1 nV/√Hz bounds any collector-current 1/f to $fS_{Ic}/I_C\lesssim1.1\times10^{-18}$ A (M2).

**(d) JFET 1/f origin, and why it is in $e_n$.** JFET 1/f is a fluctuation of the channel conductance (drain current): carrier trapping at deep levels in the depletion regions and at the surface (generation–recombination Lorentzians, RTS; Gotti T10, pp. 20–21; Kandiah, J7; Llacer and Meier, J2), on top of the mobility (Hooge) noise present in any semiconductor (Hooge P4, p. 1926; Fleetwood P5 argues number fluctuations usually dominate in devices). Because it modulates the drain current it is referred to the gate as a **voltage** ($e_{n,f}^2=S_{ID,f}/g_m^2$; Leach T8 Eqs. (64)–(65)). The gate current is the leakage of a reverse-biased junction; its noise is shot noise, flat to at least 10 mHz in a measured IF3602 (Märki et al., J5, Fig. 6(b)), with no 1/f series component in Llacer and Meier's 2N4416 (J2). The BJT is the opposite case: its 1/f is in the input-port (base) current, i.e. in $i_n$ (Leach P6, Eqs. (43)–(44)).

**(e) Measured $K_F$, $A_F$, $\alpha_H$ and corners.** Kleinpenning (P1, p. 1987): $fS_{IB}/I_B$ (= $K_F$ for $A_F=1$) is $10^{-17}$–$10^{-12}$ A in conventional transistors and $10^{-17}$–$10^{-14}$ A in poly-emitter transistors; $3\times10^{-16}$–$3\times10^{-15}$ A in 15 poly-emitter devices at $I_B$ = 10 nA, $\alpha_H=2\times10^{-6}$–$2\times10^{-5}$ (p. 1986); $\alpha_H\approx10^{-8}$ in conventional Si BJTs (p. 1985); Mounib: $10^{-16}$ A (with interfacial oxide), $3\times10^{-17}$ A (without), collector $fS_{Ic}/I_C=10^{-17}$ A (p. 1987). SiGe HBT: $\alpha_H=5\times10^{-5}$ at 250 nA, $2\times10^{-3}$ at 25 µA (P3, p. 998). Discrete low-noise BJTs [derived from data sheets, M1–M2]: MAT12 $K_F\lesssim3\times10^{-17}$ A ($f_{ci}\lesssim$ 100 Hz at 1 mA); 2SB737 $f_{ci}$ ≈ 80/230/480 Hz at 0.1/1/10 mA; LM394 $f_{ci}$ ≈ 0.2–3 kHz over 1 µA–10 mA, $K_F$ ≈ 3.5 × 10⁻¹⁶ A at 1 mA; MAT03 $f_{ci}$ ≈ 1.8 kHz; OP37 140 Hz, LT1028 250 Hz typical (op-amp input stages). JFETs [derived, J8]: $K\cdot C_{iss}$ ≈ 600 nV²·pF (6 × 10⁻²⁸ J; range 150–8200 over 17 data sets), e.g. JFE150 $K$ ≈ 17–26 nV² (table, 2–5 mA) or ≈ 196 nV² (graph, 5 mA); JFE2140 ≈ 16–62 nV²; corners 12–80 Hz (TI parts), ≈ 22–30 Hz (IF3601).

**(f) Consequences for an amplifier.** The 1/f input voltage of a BJT is $S_{IB,f}(R_S+r_{bb'})^2$ (Leach P6, Eq. (38); the flicker source sits at the internal base, Fig. 10(a), Fig. 13(a)), so the $e_n$ corner at $R_S=0$ is set by $r_{bb'}\times i_{n,f}$ and the $i_n$ corner by $K_F=2qf_f(1+1/\beta)$ (Eq. (46)). [derived] Whenever $e_{n,f}=i_{n,f}r_{bb'}$, the two 1/f parts are fully correlated and must be added coherently with the $i_nR_S$ term. Stated crossovers: Leach (p. 1534) BJT better at low source resistance, FET and BJT at 1 mA equal near 3–4 kΩ, flicker neglected, with the remark that FETs usually have more flicker noise; AN106 (X1) JFETs better from about 5 kΩ up; LT1028 (M3) current noise exceeds resistor noise at 10 Hz above about 1 kΩ. [derived, Y(d)] With sourced parameters, a BJT array beats the L front end below about 5–45 Ω when the JFET 1/f follows the JFE150 table, and below about 16–135 Ω when it follows the JFE150 measured curve, depending on the BJT's $i_n$ corner (100 Hz–1.8 kHz); for $A_F\approx1$ these crossovers hardly depend on frequency below the corners.

## Part P. BJT 1/f physics: papers read in full or in large part

### P1. Kleinpenning (1994), invited review, IEEE Trans. Electron Devices

- **Citation:** T. G. M. Kleinpenning, "Low-frequency noise in modern bipolar transistors: impact of intrinsic transistor and parasitic series resistances," *IEEE Trans. Electron Devices* 41(11), 1981–1991 (Nov. 1994), invited paper.
- **DOI:** [10.1109/16.333815](https://doi.org/10.1109/16.333815). DOI verified (PDF: TU/e repository cover sheet) and (Crossref); doi.org redirects to IEEE Xplore document 333815.
- **File:** `bjt_1f/Kleinpenning_1994_LF_Noise_Modern_Bipolar_Transistors_IEEE_TED.pdf` (final published version, TU/e Research Portal, https://pure.tue.nl/ws/files/1376103/Metis122824.pdf). PDF p. 1 is the repository cover; printed p. 1981 = PDF p. 2.
- **Access:** printed pp. 1981, 1983–1987, 1989–1990 read as rendered images; p. 1988 located and read by text extraction only; p. 1982 (measuring circuits, Fig. 1, general formulas (1)–(11)) viewed but not transcribed; reference list (p. 1991) by text.
- **Contributions:**
  - p. 1981 (abstract, introduction): at low forward currents the LF noise comes from spontaneous fluctuations in the base and collector currents; at higher currents the parasitic series resistances and their noise matter. Until 1990 the accepted LF sources were between emitter and collector and between emitter and base; a base–collector source is negligible because the junction is reverse biased.
  - p. 1983, Eqs. (12)–(14), (17): terminal noise in terms of $S_{Ib}$ (base-current source), $S_{Ic}$ (collector-current source) and $S_{Vr}$ (series-resistance voltage noise), with $Z_0=r_\pi+r_b+(\beta+1)r_e$. $r_b$ consists of a part outside the emitter ($r_{b1}$) and under the emitter ($r_{b2}$), so $r_b$ and its fluctuations are effective quantities; current crowding negligible if $I_Br_{b2}<kT/q$. Eq. (17), common-emitter output with external base resistor: $S_{VC}/R_C^2=S_{Ic}+\left[\frac{\beta(r_b+R_B)}{r_\pi+r_b+R_B}\right]^2\left[S_{Ib}+\frac{4kT}{r_b+R_B}\right]+\frac{4kT}{R_C}$.
  - p. 1983, Sect. IV-A: the base-current noise always contains shot noise ($S_{Ib}=2qI_B$, Eq. (18)) and 1/f noise, and occasionally generation–recombination (G-R) and burst (RTS) noise. Mobility-fluctuation (Hooge) expressions, all **linear in $I_B$**: recombination in the E–B space-charge region, $S_{Ib}=2\alpha qI_B/(3f\tau_j)$ (19); injection into a thick emitter, $S_{Ib}=\alpha qI_B/(4f\tau_n)$ (21); thin emitter with contact recombination velocity $s_n$, Eq. (23). G-R and RTS noise in the base current are associated with traps or g-r centres near the E–B space-charge region; fitted as $S_{Ib}=AI_B^k/(1+(2\pi f\tau)^2)$.
  - p. 1984, Eqs. (25)–(28): fluctuating trapping rate (number fluctuations) gives $S_{Ib}/I_B^2=S_{R_t}/R_t^2$; if the 1/f is read as a sum of G-R spectra with a suitable distribution of relaxation times, the noise **scales as $I_B^2$**.
  - p. 1984, Sect. IV-B: collector-current noise = shot ($2qI_C$, Eq. (29)) plus 1/f; mobility model, Eq. (30), proportional to $I_C$.
  - p. 1984, Sect. IV-C: $r_b=r_{bc}+r_{b1}+r_{b2}$ always has Nyquist noise and **can have 1/f, G-R and possibly RTS noise**: $S_{r_{b2}}=4\alpha r_{b2}^2/(3fN)$ for a circular emitter (Eq. (32); factor 18/5 instead of 4/3 for a rectangular one), $S_{r_b}=\alpha r_b^2/(fN_b^*)$ (33), $S_{r_e}=\alpha r_e^2/(fN_e^*)$ (34).
  - pp. 1984–1985, conventional BJTs: Pawlikiewicz et al. found $S_{IB}\propto I_B$ at low $I_B$ and $S_{IB}=AI_B+BI_B^k$, $k\approx2$, at higher $I_B$ (Fig. 2(a)), attributed to series-resistance noise via Eq. (37); **$\alpha_H\approx10^{-8}$** for both base and collector 1/f; at $I_C$ = 2 mA an emitter series resistance $r_e\approx12$ Ω explains a fourfold low collector noise. Zhu et al. (microwave npn): $S_{IC}=AI_C+BI_C^4$ (Eq. (39)), $\alpha_H\sim10^{-8}$; $S_{IC}/I_C$ linear in $I_C^3$ (Fig. 2(b)).
  - pp. 1985–1986, poly-emitter BJTs (Kleinpenning and Holden): spectra 1/f plus white with **corner frequencies 10 Hz–10 kHz**; change of current dependence near $I_E\approx30$ µA (Fig. 3, p. 1986); in the common-collector circuit $S_{VE}^{1/f}=[r_\pi/(\beta+1)]^2S_{Ib}^{1/f}$ (42), with $S_{Ib}\propto I_B$. About 15 devices at 1 Hz and $I_B$ = 10 nA: $S_{Ib}=3\times10^{-24}$–$3\times10^{-23}$ A²/Hz, i.e. **$fS_{Ib}/I_B=3\times10^{-16}$–$3\times10^{-15}$ A, $\alpha=2\times10^{-6}$–$2\times10^{-5}$**; above $I_E$ = 100 µA, $S_{VE}\propto I_E^2$ (resistance fluctuations in $r_e$ or $r_b$, $\alpha=10^{-6}$–$10^{-3}$).
  - p. 1986, RTS: removable in the CC circuit by choosing $R_B=r_\pi/\beta-r_b$ (Fig. 4(a)), hence located in the base current; step $\Delta I_{B,RTS}\approx20$ nA, nearly independent of $I_E$ (Fig. 4(b)), compared with $q/\tau\approx10$ nA for one excess hole in the emitter (trapping in the emitter). Pong-Fei Lu: RTS steps ≥ 15, 7 and 1 nA (Eq. (45)).
  - p. 1987: Pong-Fei Lu (devices without burst noise): $S_{Ib}^{1/f}\propto I_B^2$ for 0.5–10 µA; $fS_{Ib}/I_B=5\times10^{-16}$–$3\times10^{-15}$ A at 1–5 µA. **Summary: $fS_{Ib}/I_B$ is $10^{-17}$–$10^{-12}$ A in conventional transistors and $10^{-17}$–$10^{-14}$ A in poly-emitter transistors**, so the claim that poly emitters are noisier is called "questionable". Siabi-Shahrivar et al.: input noise at $I_E$ = 0.2 mA $\propto A_e^{-1/2}$, consistent with 1/f in $r_b$: $E_n^2=S_{Vr}=\alpha r_b^2I_B^2/(fN_b^*)\propto1/A_e$ (46). Mounib et al. ($A_e$ = 1000 × 10 µm²): $S_{IB}=aI_B+bI_B^4$ (47); $fS_{Ib}/I_B\approx10^{-16}$ A with an interfacial oxide and ≈ 3 × 10⁻¹⁷ A without; collector $fS_{Ic}/I_C=10^{-17}$ A below 10 µA for both.
  - p. 1988 (text): Mounib et al. found that defects at the Si–SiO₂ interface at the **periphery of the emitter–base junction** strongly affect the base-current noise and barely the collector noise (consistent with shorter minority-carrier lifetime, Eq. (21)).
  - pp. 1989–1990, HBTs: GaAs/AlGaAs, $S_{Ib}=C_BI_B^k/f$ with $k\approx1.6$ and $fS_{Ib}/I_B=10^{-14}$–$10^{-12}$ A, decades above poly-emitter BJTs; collector $k\approx1.3$.
  - p. 1990, conclusions: in high-quality Si BJTs the LF noise is the base-current 1/f, normally proportional to $I_B$ (collector 1/f proportional to $I_C$), as the mobility model predicts; deviations at high currents come from series resistances; some devices show RTS from traps in the emitter; whether the 1/f is mobility or trapping noise cannot be decided from current dependences alone.

### P2. Markus and Kleinpenning (1995), poly-emitter BJTs, IEEE Trans. Electron Devices

- **Citation:** H. A. W. Markus, T. G. M. Kleinpenning, "Low-frequency noise in polysilicon emitter bipolar transistors," *IEEE Trans. Electron Devices* 42(4), 720–727 (Apr. 1995).
- **DOI:** [10.1109/16.372077](https://doi.org/10.1109/16.372077). DOI verified (PDF cover sheet) and (Crossref); doi.org → IEEE Xplore document 372077.
- **File:** `bjt_1f/Markus_Kleinpenning_1995_LF_Noise_Polysilicon_Emitter_BJT_IEEE_TED.pdf` (TU/e, https://research.tue.nl/p/file/document/1580586/Metis122897.pdf).
- **Access:** printed pp. 720–723 and 725–726 read as images, Figs. 3 and 9 enlarged; pp. 724 and 727 not read.
- **Contributions:**
  - p. 720: Philips poly-emitter npn devices from seven wafers, emitter lengths 0.8–100 µm and widths 0.3–100 µm, native (10–15 Å) or grown (3.8, 7.5, 8 Å) interfacial oxide. Literature summary: $S_{Ib}\propto I_B$ (Kleinpenning), $\propto I_B^2$ (Pong-Fei Lu; Wai Shing Lau, transparency fluctuations of the interfacial oxide), both regimes (Pawlikiewicz); 1/f strongly tied to the surface treatment before poly deposition (Siabi-Shahrivar; Mounib).
  - p. 721: noise model Eqs. (1)–(7); $r_eA_E$ = 40–160 Ω·µm²; measured 0.1–100 µA base current, 10 µA–10 mA collector current, 1 Hz–100 kHz; 1/f over 1 to 5 decades in all devices.
  - p. 722, Fig. 3: $S_{Ib}$(1 Hz) against $I_B$ for $A_E$ = 0.8 to 10⁴ µm² (7.5 Å oxide): **$\propto I_B^2$**, except large-area devices at low current (**$\propto I_B$**, p. 723: mobility fluctuations may dominate there). Fig. 4: **$S_{Ib}/I_B^2\propto1/A_E$**, ≈ 10⁻⁹ Hz⁻¹ at 1 µm² falling to ≈ 10⁻¹²–10⁻¹³ Hz⁻¹ at 10⁴ µm² (1 Hz). Eq. (9): for uncorrelated, homogeneously distributed sources $S_{IB}\propto A_ES_{JB}=I_B^kA_E^{1-k}$; $k=2$ found, so the sources are **spread uniformly over the emitter area** (interface), not at the perimeter. Fig. 5: $S_{Ib}/I_B^2$ grows with oxide thickness, ≈ 4 × 10⁻¹² Hz⁻¹ (3.8 Å) to ≈ 2 × 10⁻¹¹ Hz⁻¹ (8 Å) for $A_E$ = 8.8 × 48 µm²; grown oxide less noisy than native.
  - p. 722, Eq. (8): at high current $S_{VE}\approx r_b^2S_{Ib}+I_E^2S_{re}+I_B^2S_{rb}$; small-emitter devices show $S_{VE}$ up to three orders above $r_b^2S_{Ib}$ (series-resistance fluctuations).
  - p. 723, Eq. (10): transparency-fluctuation (tunnelling) model of the interfacial oxide.
  - p. 725: attributing the high-current noise to base-resistance fluctuations would need $\alpha$ = 10⁻²–10⁻¹ ("quite high"); emitter-resistance fluctuations dominate; Fig. 8, $S_{re}/r_e^2$ linear in $S_{Ib}/I_B^2$.
  - p. 726, Sect. V, **reverse-bias stress**: reverse biasing the E–B junction increases the non-ideal base current and the noise generated in the junction. Fig. 9 ($A_E$ = 100 × 100 µm², 7.5 Å): after 30 min at 4, 6 and 7 V reverse bias, the branch **proportional to $I_B$** (low current) rises, at $I_B$ ≈ 0.5 µA from ≈ 1 × 10⁻²⁴ (unstressed, extrapolated) to ≈ 3.5, 5 and 6 × 10⁻²⁴ A²/Hz at 1 Hz, while the $I_B^2$ branch (≈ 2.5 × 10⁻²⁰ A²/Hz at 130 µA) is unchanged: two different sources.
  - p. 726, conclusions: 1/f in $S_{Ib}$ and in $S_{re}$; base-current sources homogeneous over the emitter area; 1/f increases with interfacial-oxide thickness; low oxide transparency reduces the mobility-fluctuation part.

### P3. Bruce, Vandamme and Rydberg (1999), base and collector noise coherence, IEEE Trans. Electron Devices

- **Citation:** S. P. O. Bruce, L. K. J. Vandamme, A. Rydberg, "Measurement of low-frequency base and collector current noise and coherence in SiGe heterojunction bipolar transistors using transimpedance amplifiers," *IEEE Trans. Electron Devices* 46(5), 993–1000 (May 1999).

- **File:** `bjt_1f/Bruce_Vandamme_Rydberg_1999_Base_Collector_Noise_Coherence_SiGe_HBT_IEEE_TED.pdf` (TU/e, https://research.tue.nl/p/file/document/1589476/Metis132948.pdf).
- **Access:** printed pp. 993 and 995–998 read as images; pp. 994, 999–1000 not read.
- **Contributions:**
  - Method (p. 995): transimpedance amplifiers (SR570; Brookdeal 5002, the SR570 being found insufficient) on base and collector leads measure both noise currents simultaneously over a wide range of bias resistances; frequencies 20, 25 or 170 Hz chosen to avoid mains harmonics.
  - Fig. 3 (p. 996; 20 µm² device, $R_S$ = 10 kΩ, $I_B$ = 1 µA): base-lead and collector-lead spectra $\propto1/f^{0.8}$ below 10 kHz, **coherence ≈ 1**: one common source ($S_{IB}$, $S_{rb}$ or $S_{re}$).
  - Fig. 4 (24 µm², $I_B$ = 2 µA, 170 Hz): collector noise $\propto R_S^2$ for 300 Ω ≤ $R_S$ ≤ $r_\pi$, so $S_{IB}$ is the single dominant source; coherence drops at low $R_S$, revealing an extra source $S_X=4.4\times10^{-21}$ A²/Hz at 170 Hz (Eq. (9); candidate: the collector-current noise); **$r_b+r_e=28$ Ω extracted from noise** (Eqs. (11)–(12)).
  - Fig. 5 (p. 997; 25 Hz, $V_{CE}$ = 1.5 V, $R_S$ = 100 kΩ): **$S_{IB}\propto I_B^{1.7}$ from 2 nA to 25 µA**; below 250 nA the frequency dependence is weaker than 1/f. Table I: $h_{fe}$ from noise against Gummel-plot values.
  - p. 997–998: mobility-fluctuation model Eq. (17), $S_{IB}=2\alpha qI_BD_p/(fW_E^2)$, gives **$\alpha=5\times10^{-5}$ at $I_B$ = 250 nA and $2\times10^{-3}$ at 25 µA** (25 Hz); number-fluctuation (surface recombination velocity) model Eq. (18), $S_I/I^2=C\,S_s$, predicts the square law and fits the near-square law observed.
  - p. 998, conclusion: the dominant source is in the base–emitter junction, emitter side ($W_E$ region), **not in $r_b$ or $r_e$**; coherence was essential to locate it.
  - Relevance: direct evidence that the base and collector 1/f seen at the terminals come from one base-current source (fully correlated), and a noise-based $r_b+r_e$ extraction method.

### P4. Hooge (1994), 1/f noise sources, IEEE Trans. Electron Devices

- **Citation:** F. N. Hooge, "1/f noise sources," *IEEE Trans. Electron Devices* 41(11), 1926–1935 (Nov. 1994).
- **DOI:** [10.1109/16.333808](https://doi.org/10.1109/16.333808). DOI verified (PDF cover sheet) and (Crossref); doi.org → IEEE Xplore document 333808.
- **File:** `bjt_1f/Hooge_1994_1f_Noise_Sources_IEEE_TED.pdf` (TU/e, https://research.tue.nl/p/file/document/1562485/Metis122802.pdf).
- **Access:** printed pp. 1926–1930 and 1934 read as images; pp. 1931–1933 located by text only. The paper contains no BJT- or JFET-specific material.
- **Contributions:**

## Part J. JFET 1/f, the counterpart

### J1. Origin of JFET 1/f and why it appears in $e_n$ (sources combined)

- Mechanisms: G-R noise and RTS from deep traps in the depletion regions and at the surface, with single-trap Lorentzians visible in good devices (Gotti, T10 pp. 20–21: 2SK146, SNJ3600; Kandiah 1994, J7; Llacer and Meier, J2); the Hooge mobility noise present in all semiconductors (P4, p. 1926; $\alpha\approx10^{-4}$ in perfect material); Fleetwood (P5) argues number fluctuations by defect charge exchange usually dominate in devices.
- Referral: the fluctuation is in the channel conductance, i.e. the drain current; divided by $g_m$ it is an input **voltage** source: $e_{n,f}^2=K_f/(4Kf)$ for the square-law JFET with $I_{fd}^2=K_fI_D/f$ (Leach T8, Eqs. (64)–(65)), independent of $I_D$ in that model. Area scaling $A_f\propto1/(\text{gate area})$, $K_f=A_fC_{gs}\approx10^{-27}$ J for JFETs (Radeka T1 pp. 459–460; Spieler T3 p. 9; Gotti T10 p. 22).
- Gate current: shot noise of the junction leakage, $i_n^2=2qI_G$ (T3, T8 Eq. (66)); measured flat to ≈ 10 mHz in an IF3602 (J5); Llacer and Meier's four-term model has a pure shot parallel term and saw no 1/f series component (J2); Stocker and Jones (J7): input current noise mostly white shot noise, excess LF components in some devices. Impact ionization raises $I_G$ to nA above $V_{DG}$ of about 6 V (TI parts) to 12–16 V (LSK389) (J8), still negligible against 1–100 Ω sources.
- Bias dependence (measured, against the $I_D$-independent model): JFE150 sub-5 Hz noise lower at 2 mA than at 5 mA (curves cross near 12–15 Hz); JFE2140 at 10 V shows more 1/f than at about 4.7 V (J6, J8); 2SK3557 more 1/f at 7.5 V / 14.6 mA than at 1.9–3.3 V (J3, Fig. 4 p. 534, text p. 535).

### S4. Calibrating RB/RBM/IRB, KF and AF from data sheets [derived, using Leach P6 and S2]

1. **Base resistance at the operating point.** $r_{bb'}=e_{n,w}^2/(4kT)-r_e/2$ from the white $e_n$ at $R_S\approx0$ (Leach Eq. (43)); or from total noise against $R_S$ (Bruce P3, Eqs. (11)–(12)). Choose RB, RBM, IRB so that the S2 formula gives this value at the operating $I_B$; for a fixed operating point RB = RBM = $r_{bb'}$ with IRB unset is the simplest. Do not use RF-measured $r_{bb'}$ (Toshiba at 100 MHz, Rohm at 30 MHz): the 2SB737's noise floor implies ≈ 8–9 Ω against its 2 Ω RF value (M2).
2. **$\beta(I_C)$** via BF, ISE, NE (and IKF): the flicker source uses the total base current, including the ISE leakage component (S2).
3. **AF and KF from $i_n(f)$ at two or more currents:** $KF\cdot I_B^{AF}=f\,[i_n^2(f)-2qI_B]$; AF from the slope against $I_B$ (or from how the corner moves: $f_{ci}\propto I_B^{AF-1}$); for AF = 1, $KF=2qf_{ci}$, or $2qf_{ci}(1+1/\beta)$ with Leach's Eq. (46). Values available [derived, M1–M2]: LM394 AF ≈ 1.3, KF(AF = 1) ≈ 3.5 × 10⁻¹⁶ A at 1 mA; 2SB737 AF ≈ 1.4, ≈ 7 × 10⁻¹⁷ A at 1 mA; MAT03 AF ≈ 1, ≈ 6 × 10⁻¹⁶ A; MAT12 ≲ 3 × 10⁻¹⁷ A. These lie at the low end of Kleinpenning's IC ranges (P1).
4. **If only $e_n(f)$ at $R_S\approx0$ is published** (MAT12, SSM2212, LS parts): KF fitted to $e_n$ alone would assume $e_{n,f}=i_{n,f}r_{bb'}$ and can produce an unrealistic $i_n$ (MAT12 table: corner ≈ 40 kHz). Fit KF to an $i_n$-sensitive datum (total noise against high $R_S$, e.g. MAT12 Fig. 4) and represent any remaining $e_n$ 1/f by a separate 1/f voltage source in series with the base.
5. **Paralleled devices:** use the instance multiplier $m$ (S2).

## Part Y. Synthesis

### Y(a). Scaling laws and reported ranges

- **Where the noise is:** base current (input port) in Si BJTs (P1, P3); collector-current 1/f smaller (P3; [derived] MAT03 bound $fS_{Ic}/I_C\lesssim10^{-18}$ A at 1 mA); series-resistance 1/f at high currents (P1 p. 1986, P2 p. 725).
- **Current exponent $A_F$ (= Leach's $m$):** 1 (mobility model; P1, conventional and poly at low current), ≈ 2 (number fluctuations, poly-emitter interfacial oxide; P2, Q11, Q13), 1.6–1.7 (HBTs; P1, P3), 1–2 (Q7), 1–3 (P6). Discrete low-noise parts [derived]: ≈ 1 (MAT03), 1.2–1.4 (LM394, 2SB737), 1.5–1.7 (2N5088/2N930, X1).
- **Area and perimeter:** $S_{IB}/I_B^2\propto1/A_E$ for uniformly distributed sources (P2 Fig. 4; Q11; Q13), perimeter terms for large perimeter/area or surface-dominated devices (Q2, Q3, Q13; P1 p. 1988). [derived] For $N$ identical devices sharing a fixed total $I_B$: $S_{IB,tot}\propto N^{1-A_F}$, so paralleling lowers the base-current 1/f only when $A_F>1$ (by $1/N$ for $A_F=2$).
- **Magnitude:** $fS_{IB}/I_B$ ($K_F$ for $A_F=1$) $10^{-17}$–$10^{-12}$ A conventional, $10^{-17}$–$10^{-14}$ A poly-emitter, $10^{-14}$–$10^{-12}$ A GaAs HBT (P1); discrete low-noise parts $\lesssim3\times10^{-17}$ to ≈ 6 × 10⁻¹⁶ A [derived]. $\alpha_H$: ≈ 10⁻⁸ (conventional, P1), 2 × 10⁻⁶–2 × 10⁻⁵ (poly, P1), 5 × 10⁻⁵–2 × 10⁻³ (SiGe, P3), ≈ 10⁻⁴ in homogeneous material (P4).
- **Corners:** $f_{ci}=K_FI_B^{A_F-1}/(2q)$: 10⁻¹⁷ A ↔ 31 Hz, 10⁻¹⁶ A ↔ 310 Hz, 10⁻¹⁵ A ↔ 3.1 kHz; current-independent for $A_F=1$, rising as $I_B^{A_F-1}$ otherwise. Reported: MAT12 ≲ 100 Hz [derived]; 2SB737 80–480 Hz (0.1–10 mA); LM394 0.2–3 kHz (1 µA–10 mA); MAT03 ≈ 1.8 kHz; OP37 140 Hz; LT1028 250 Hz (≤ 800 Hz); poly-emitter IC devices 10 Hz–10 kHz (P1). $e_n$ corner at $R_S=0$ [derived from Leach Eqs. (43)–(44)]: $f_{ce}=\frac{K_FI_B^{A_F}r_{bb'}^2}{4kTr_{bb'}+2kTr_e}$, which grows with $I_C$ and $r_{bb'}$ (towards $K_FI_Br_{bb'}/(4kT)$ when $r_{bb'}\gg r_e/2$). Reported $e_n$ corners: ≈ 1–3 Hz at 1 mA for MAT03, MAT02, LS310 and the MAT12 graph, ≈ 25 Hz for the MAT12 table, ≈ 35 Hz for LS310 at 100 µA; OP37 2.7 Hz, LT1028 3.5 Hz (14 Hz max). Above a few mA per device the 10 Hz $e_n$ rises steeply (MAT03 TPC 9: 0.82 → 5.3 nV/√Hz from 1 to 12 mA; MAT02 p. 4), so there is an optimum current per device for low-frequency work.
- **$r_{bb'}$:** its thermal noise sets $e_n$ at high current; it can carry its own 1/f (P1 Eqs. (32)–(33), (46)), seen as $S_V\propto I_B^2$ at high current; RF-measured values understate the noise-equivalent value (M2).
- **Correlation:** [derived] the 1/f parts of $e_n$ and $i_n$ are fully correlated when both come from the base-current source; total $S_{IB,f}(R_S+r_{bb'})^2$ (P6 Eq. (38); Q1; P3 coherence ≈ 1). Adding them in quadrature underestimates the noise by up to 3 dB when $R_S\approx r_{bb'}$.
- **Burst noise:** traps or g-r centres near the E–B space-charge region and metallic impurities (P1 p. 1983, P6 p. 1517, Q2); steps of 1–20 nA in $I_B$ (P1 p. 1986); less in high-gain devices (Q4); popcorn confined below ≈ 15 Hz at low current density (Q6); small devices show individual G-R centres (Q15). No data sheet quantifies it.
- **Degradation:** E–B reverse breakdown or reverse-bias stress raises 1/f (Q3; P6 p. 1517; P2 Fig. 9: ×3.5–6 in the low-current branch after 4–7 V for 30 min); hot-carrier stress creates RTS (Q14, Q10). Internal B–E protection diodes: MAT12, SSM2212, MAT02, MAT03, SSM2220, LM394 (the LM394 sheet calls B–E breakdown the most common field failure of matched pairs, pp. 1–2); none in the THAT 300 or Linear Systems parts.

### Y(c). JFET counterpart (J8)

- Input-referred 1/f per device: JFE150 $K$ ≈ 17.5 (2 mA) to 26 nV² (5 mA) from the table, ≈ 56–196 nV² from Fig. 6-9; JFE2140 ≈ 16–62 nV² (data sheet), ≈ 9.5–16.7 nV² measured at 7 mA and ≈ 4.7 V (J6); IF3601 ≈ 2.5 nV² (650–890 pF); LSK170 ≈ 11.5; LSK389 ≈ 77; BF862 ≈ 239 (measured); JFE2325 ≈ 10⁴ (0.85 pF).
- Scaling: $K\cdot C_{iss}$ ≈ 600 nV²·pF median (150–8200), close to $K\propto1/C$ (area); 1/f rises with $I_D$ and $V_{DS}$ in the TI and 2SK3557 data, contrary to the textbook $I_D$-independence; below 1 Hz measured preamplifiers fall faster than 1/f.
- Gate current: shot noise only (J5, J7); 41 fA/√Hz of the L front end's bias resistors gives 4 pV/√Hz into 100 Ω, i.e. the JFET front end's whole low-frequency penalty is in $e_n$.
- L front end, JFET part only (differential): 0.30–0.33 (white), 0.73–0.78 (10 Hz), 2.1–5.7 nV/√Hz (1 Hz) depending on which TI 1/f data are believed.

### Y(d). BJT against JFET below 100 Ω: implications and open questions

**Model [derived].** Per side, $N$ BJTs at $I_C$ each, series source resistance $R_S$ per side, source thermal noise excluded:
$$e_{amp}^2=\frac{4kTr_{bb'}+2kTr_e}{N}+N\,S_{IB,1}(f)\left(R_S+\frac{r_{bb'}}{N}\right)^2,\qquad S_{IB,1}(f)=2qI_B\left(1+\frac{f_{ci}}{f}\right),$$
(Leach P6 Eqs. (38), (43)–(44) applied to $N$ devices: each device's base noise flows through $R_S$ and its own $r_{bb'}$), against the L side $e_{amp}^2=(e_w^2+K/f)/12$ with $e_w$ = 0.75 nV/√Hz per JFE150. Differential values are $\sqrt2$ times larger for both; the crossovers are unchanged.

- With $A_F\approx1$ the BJT's $i_n$ and the JFET's $e_n$ both scale as $1/f$ below their corners, so **the crossover resistance is nearly independent of frequency from 1 to 100 Hz**; it is set by the ratio of the BJT's base-current 1/f to the JFET's voltage 1/f.
- The resulting range, **≈ 5–45 Ω** against the JFE150 table and **≈ 16–130 Ω** against the measured JFE150 curve, brackets the first estimate of 20–80 Ω. At $R_S$ ≤ 3–5 Ω the BJT array is better in every case (at 3 Ω: 0.21–0.40 nV/√Hz per side at 10 Hz and 0.25–1.10 at 1 Hz, against 0.50–1.30 and 1.43–4.05 for the L side); at 10 Ω it is better except for the MAT03-class corner of 1.8 kHz against the table-based JFET 1/f. At 100 Ω the BJT array loses unless its $i_n$ corner is ≤ 100 Hz and the JFET 1/f is at the high end.
- Leach's optimum count, generalized [derived]: for the MAT12-class device with $f_{ci}$ = 100 Hz the best $N$ at 10 Hz is ≈ 34 / 11 / 3 for $R_S$ = 10 / 30 / 100 Ω (0.21 / 0.36 / 0.66 nV/√Hz per side); with $f_{ci}$ = 1.5 kHz it is ≈ 10 / 3 / 1 (0.45 / 0.78 / 1.41). Fewer devices are optimal at low frequency than for white noise, because the base-current 1/f grows with $N$.
- A transformer ($n^2R_S=V_n/I_n$, T8) moves a 1–10 Ω source into the JFET's favourable range and avoids the BJT's base-current 1/f altogether; the SR554 (0.1 nV/√Hz, 0.1 Hz–40 kHz) shows the practical result, but its 1/f was not documented in the sources read.

**Uncertainties.**
- No in-production BJT pair publishes $i_n(f)$; the $i_n$ corner of the MAT12/SSM2212 (≲ 100 Hz) rests on one graph (Fig. 4) at one frequency, and conflicts with the same data sheet's 10 Hz $e_n$ (1.6 nV/√Hz typ).
- The JFET 1/f of the TI parts differs by a factor of 2–7 in power between table, curve and p-p spec (J8); the crossover moves by ≈ 3× with it.
- Device-to-device spread, burst noise and screening of BJTs are not quantified in any data sheet; small-emitter devices are dominated by individual G-R centres (Q15), and an array of $N$ devices has $N$ chances of including a noisy one.
- The $f_{ci}$ derived from TPC 10 for the MAT03 depends on how the plot is normalized (single device against pair).
- Collector-current 1/f and series-resistance 1/f at high current are not in the Gummel–Poon model (S2); the steep rise of $e_n$(10 Hz) above ≈ 6 mA per device (MAT03, MAT02) must be avoided by keeping the per-device current near 1–3 mA.
- BJT input capacitance at mA currents ($C_\pi$) is larger per device than the JFE150's 24 pF; whether this matters depends on the feedback bootstrapping in the current-feedback topology.
- Slow thermal drift of $V_{BE}$ (≈ −2 mV/K per transistor, differential through imperfect thermal coupling) can dominate the 0.1–1 Hz region of BJT pairs (inferred from the MAT12 p-p spec and Fig. 3; not documented as such in the sources).
